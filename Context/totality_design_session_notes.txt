PRIORITY 1 — REST SYSTEM (build first)
========================================================
This was already designed in an earlier session and is considered fully
locked, with two corrections made THIS session (marked below).

Trigger — two supported entry points:
1. Ability-based: equip "Rest" from the abilities menu, can activate anywhere
   (BG3 camping style).
2. Bed-based: right-click a bed -> popup (Short Rest / Long Rest / Cancel).
   Long Rest hooks vanilla sleep via mixin.

REST RESOLUTION IN MULTIPLAYER (resolved this session — important for
avoiding time-skip conflicts when not everyone wants to rest at once):
  - LONG REST: uses vanilla SLEEP mechanics as the default path. If not
    every player wants to sleep at the same time, a player can instead
    just occupy/use the bed for the REQUIRED DURATION (e.g. 4 hours for
    an Elf-equivalent Ancestry, matching the real 5e Trance rule below)
    WITHOUT actually skipping game time — this means Long Rest does NOT
    have to force a server-wide night-skip just because one player wants
    to rest; only a genuine vanilla sleep vote/majority should trigger the
    actual time-skip, otherwise resting just passes that player's own
    required duration in real time while the world keeps ticking normally
    for everyone else.
  - COMFORT BONUS (new, Skyrim-inspired): sleeping in a bed, or some
    other sufficiently "comfortable" resting spot, should grant a small
    POSITIVE effect (mirrors Skyrim's Well Rested/Lover's Comfort-style
    bonuses) — exact bonus not yet decided, just the concept confirmed:
    resting somewhere genuinely comfortable should feel meaningfully
    better than resting on the ground.
  - SHORT REST: the player always just WAITS OUT the full required
    duration in real time (no time-skip option at all) — this avoids
    messing with world time in multiplayer, since Short Rest is short
    enough that skipping it isn't necessary the way Long Rest's 8 hours
    would be. FUTURE POLISH (not yet built): proper animations for
    whatever activity is chosen during a Short Rest (reading, meditating,
    etc.) rather than just standing/waiting — flagged as a nice-to-have
    visual pass for later, not a blocker on the mechanic itself.
  - ANCESTRY/RACE-SPECIFIC REST OUTLIERS (confirmed real 5e reference
    rules found this session, to use as Totality's baseline where
    relevant Ancestries exist):
      - ELF-STYLE TRANCE (real 5e rule): Elves meditate semiconsciously
        for 4 hours instead of sleeping 8, and this counts as a FULL Long
        Rest. Important: they are SEMICONSCIOUS, not unconscious, the
        whole time — aware of their surroundings — and they DO still
        technically "dream" during Trance (described as reflexive mental
        exercises rather than true dreaming). Applying this to Totality:
        an Elf-equivalent Ancestry should get the shorter 4-hour Long Rest
        duration, could plausibly still trigger the NIGHTMARE rest-event
        (since something dream-like still happens), and being semi-
        conscious rather than unconscious could reasonably grant a
        Perception/detection bonus during the rest window (better odds
        against Ambush/Theft) compared to a fully-unconscious sleeper.
      - CONSTRUCT-STYLE REST (real 5e rule, Warforged/Autognome
        reference, "Sentry's Rest"): constructs don't need sleep and are
        immune to exhaustion from lack of rest, and magic can't put them
        to sleep — BUT they are NOT exempt from spending the Long Rest
        DURATION. The actual rule requires spending at least 6 hours in
        an INACTIVE, MOTIONLESS state instead of sleeping (a "power
        down"/standby state, not unconsciousness, not skipped entirely).
        Applying this to Totality's Automaton-style Ancestry (if/when
        built): should still go through the full Rest duration like
        anyone else, framed narratively as powering down rather than
        sleeping, and — matching Stefan's own instinct — NO DREAMS means
        the NIGHTMARE rest-event genuinely CANNOT apply to them, while
        Ambush/Theft/Perception Check/Visitor still could (they're still
        present/vulnerable during standby, just not unconscious).
  - OTHER OUTLIERS (Vampire needing a coffin, Werewolves potentially
    transforming and going to hunt during sleep, etc.) EXPLICITLY
    DEFERRED — Stefan wants to design these once those specific
    Ancestry/curse systems are actually being built, rather than
    speculating on their rest behavior now. Noted as a reminder that Rest
    will likely need per-Ancestry/per-curse override hooks eventually
    (matching the Elf/Construct pattern above), even though the specific
    content for Vampire/Werewolf is not decided yet.

Architecture:
  api/rpg/rest/
      RestType.java     — enum SHORT, LONG
      RestManager.java  — handles rest logic, fires events
      RestEvents.java   — onShortRest(player) / onLongRest(player) hooks
  Classes/subclasses hook into these events for their own recovery (Rage,
  Ki, Arcane Recovery, etc.) rather than RestManager special-casing per class.

Recovery table (D&D 2024 rules baseline):
  HP               | Short: spend Hit Dice (roll class die + CON mod)   | Long: full
  Stamina          | Short: nothing                                     | Long: full
  Mana — Warlock   | Short: full (Pact Magic)                           | Long: full
  Mana — Monk      | Short: full Ki, requires 30 min in-world meditation| Long: full
  Mana — Wizard    | Short: partial — Arcane Recovery, once per Long
                     Rest period, recovers slots up to half class level | Long: full
  Mana — Barbarian | Short: nothing                                     | Long: full
  Rage charges     | Short: +1 charge                                   | Long: all charges
  Hit Dice pool    | Short: nothing                                     | Long: ALL restored
                     (2024 rule — NOT half, that was the old 2014 rule)
  Spell slots      | Short: Warlock only                                | Long: all casters
  Class abilities  | Short: some, per-class                             | Long: all
  Magic item charges| Short: some items                                 | Long: all

Outdoor Long Rest risk (build alongside or shortly after core Rest):
  - Resting outdoors carries risk; resting in a proper bed does not (or
    risk is much lower). Makes wilderness resting feel dangerous vs. safe
    indoor resting. CORRECTION/EXPANSION: this is not just "ambush" — the
    full planned event pool has FIVE distinct outcomes, only one of which
    (Ambush) has real mechanical detail worked out so far this session:
      1. AMBUSH — enemies attack while resting. Fully designed this
         session: location-keyed AND narrative-flag-keyed encounter
         tables (cemetery -> undead/grave-robbers, killed-a-noble flag ->
         assassins/thugs), routed through Dialogue for human/sapient
         ambushers (demand -> comply/Persuasion-roll/fight/Stealth-
         escape choices) while non-sapient ambushers (undead) skip
         dialogue and attack immediately.
      2. THEFT — items stolen during the Long Rest. No mechanical detail
         yet: does the player get any chance to notice/prevent it (a
         Perception check, similar to #4 below), or is it a silent loss
         discovered only on waking? Not decided.
      3. NIGHTMARE — a negative effect applied to the player. Not decided
         what kind of effect (a debuff, a narrative/sanity-style
         consequence, a plot hook) or what determines whether it
         triggers.
      4. PERCEPTION CHECK — player detects something nearby (implies this
         is a "near-miss" or warning-only outcome, distinct from a full
         Ambush actually landing) — not decided what happens on success/
         failure of the check itself, or what the "something" typically
         is.
      5. VISITOR — a friendly or neutral encounter, NOT everything that
         can happen during rest is bad. Not decided what a Visitor
         actually offers (dialogue, a small quest hook, a trade
         opportunity, lore) or how it's differentiated from a normal
         daytime NPC encounter.
  - REST-TYPE SPLIT (new decision): outdoor rest-risk events do NOT apply
    identically to Short Rest and Long Rest — during a Long Rest (~8
    hours) the player is genuinely asleep/unconscious for the duration;
    during a Short Rest (~30 min) the player is often doing something
    active (Arcane Recovery study, Ki meditation, etc.) rather than fully
    unconscious, so alertness differs meaningfully between the two:
      - NIGHTMARE is LONG-REST-EXCLUSIVE — it requires actual dreaming/
        sleep, which doesn't apply to an active Short Rest.
      - AMBUSH, THEFT, PERCEPTION CHECK, and VISITOR can all apply to
        BOTH rest types, but the player's effective detection odds should
        be noticeably BETTER during a Short Rest than a Long Rest (less
        vulnerable when semi-aware/active vs. fully asleep) — exact
        mechanism (flat bonus to a roll, harder DC for the
        ambusher/thief to succeed against an alert target, etc.) not yet
        decided, but the DIRECTION (Short Rest = safer) is confirmed.
  - PASSIVE PERCEPTION (new mechanic, needed for rest-risk events and
    likely useful more broadly): rather than every detection check being
    an active roll, characters should have a PASSIVE PERCEPTION score —
    a static number derived from build (e.g. a Cleric with good Wisdom
    might sit around Passive Perception ~30 at medium levels) that acts
    as an automatic threshold: if an ambusher/thief's stealth/DC doesn't
    beat the player's Passive Perception, the player just automatically
    notices them (no active roll needed, similar to 5e's actual Passive
    Perception mechanic — 10 + relevant modifiers, used specifically so
    the DM/game doesn't need to call for a roll on every single passing
    threat). This should be the FIRST check run for rest-risk events
    (and possibly more broadly for stealth/ambush detection generally)
    before falling back to an active roll-based check in closer/more
    contested situations.
    - FUTURE EXPANSION (not yet designed, explicitly forward-looking):
      Passive Perception could be further modified by things beyond base
      stats — e.g. bonuses granted by a Warlock's specific Patron, a
      worshipped Deity, or specific completed quests. Not yet built or
      itemized, just flagged as a natural place for those systems to hook
      in later once Patrons/Deities/relevant quests exist in more detail.
  - LONG REST detection flow (refined): Passive Perception check runs
    first (automatic, no roll). If it fails, the player can still get a
    MID-REST ACTIVE PERCEPTION ROLL, but WITH DISADVANTAGE (reflecting
    being asleep and only partially rousable) as a second chance to
    notice before the event fully resolves.
  - SHORT REST SPLIT INTO TWO SUB-MODES (new): a Short Rest is no longer
    one uniform state — the player either:
      1. NORMAL SHORT REST (meditating, reading, sharpening a blade,
         Arcane Recovery study, etc.) — player is ACTIVE/AWAKE. Mid-rest
         theft/ambush detection uses a normal Perception check (no
         advantage or disadvantage).
      2. TAKING A NAP during the Short Rest — player is asleep for a
         shorter window than Long Rest. Mid-rest detection uses a
         Perception check WITH ADVANTAGE (more aware than a full Long
         Rest sleeper, since the nap is much shorter, but still asleep so
         not as alert as the Normal Short Rest mode above).
    (Ordering of alertness, most to least aware: Normal Short Rest >
    Napping Short Rest > Long Rest.)
  - FUTURE SHORT REST ACTIVITY IDEA (forward-looking, not yet built): the
    player could have IN-WORLD THINGS TO DO during a Normal Short Rest
    beyond just waiting out the timer — reading a found book is just ONE
    example (granting a quest, an ability, XP, or other reward), NOT the
    only planned activity type — other situational activities may be
    added later depending on context/location (exact other examples not
    yet decided). Some larger activities (e.g. a big book) could require
    MULTIPLE Short Rests to complete (one chapter per rest, spread across
    several rests). Not designed in detail, just flagged as a good future
    use of the "what do you actually do during a Normal Short Rest"
    question.

--- THEFT (Long/Short Rest event) — full mechanic design ---
- Detection layer: uses the Passive Perception + advantage/disadvantage
  rules above, varying by which rest sub-mode the player is in.
- CRITICAL FAILURE TIER: if the player's active perception roll (the
  fallback roll after failing Passive Perception) comes up a NATURAL 1,
  this means the player was sleeping unusually soundly and did NOT notice
  ANYTHING — this is worse than a normal failed check, and should let the
  thief take MORE/better items than a standard failed detection would
  allow (a distinct, harsher failure tier, not just "you failed, roll the
  normal loss").
- ITEM SELECTION PRIORITY ORDER: the thief targets items in a defined
  priority, not randomly:
    1. Money first — Credits/Coins are the thief's default target before
       anything else.
    2. Beyond money, WHAT ELSE the thief can take is constrained by the
       thief's own carrying capacity — see Weight-based limit below.
- WEIGHT-BASED THEFT LIMIT (ties into the still-unbuilt WEIGHT SYSTEM,
  another consumer alongside Mage Hand's carry capacity and Powerful
  Build-style race traits — see earlier dependency notes): the Thief
  should be an ACTUAL MOB ENTITY (not an abstract "you got robbed" text
  event), and their own END/Endurance stat (or whichever stat ends up
  governing carry weight) determines how much they can physically steal
  in one theft. This means a heavily-loaded target (e.g. a Barbarian
  carrying a lot of gear) can't have EVERYTHING stolen even on a bad
  detection roll — the thief is capped by their own carry capacity, not
  the victim's total inventory value. Blocked on Weight system existing
  before this can be fully implemented.
- QUEST ITEM THEFT (rare, special case): a SMALL chance (~5%) that the
  thief steals an actual QUEST ITEM, but this should be gated by a new
  STEALABLE TAG on quest items — not every quest item makes sense to be
  stolen (a "pile of shit"-tier junk quest item has no reason for a thief
  to bother with it), so quest items need an explicit stealable/not-
  stealable flag set per-item by whoever authors the quest, rather than
  ALL quest items being eligible by default.
    - If a quest item IS stolen: the player NEEDS to complete a small
      RECOVERY QUEST to get it back (new quest content, presumably
      generated/templated rather than hand-written per instance given
      how rare/random this event is).
    - Player can also attempt an INVESTIGATION CHECK to find clues
      leading to the thief/their location, as an alternative or
      supplement to however the recovery quest surfaces the thief.
- FUTURE COMPANION INTERACTION (forward-looking, depends on the not-yet-
  built Companion system): if a Companion is present during a rest, they
  should get their OWN detection roll independently of the player (a
  companion might wake and notice/wake the player even if the player's
  own check fails). Companions can ALSO have items stolen from them
  directly, separate from the player's own losses. Some stolen companion
  items could be PERSONALLY IMPORTANT to that companion specifically (not
  just generic loot) — recovering such an item for them should INCREASE
  Relationship level with that companion, giving Theft a second use as a
  Relationship-building hook, not just a punishment mechanic.
--- PERCEPTION CHECK (Long/Short Rest event) — design ---
- Not just a Perception roll specifically — this event fires from a
  RANDOM TABLE of different skill CHECK TYPES, each surfacing different
  flavor/rewards appropriate to that skill:
    - INVESTIGATION check — could reveal something usable for a later
      quest, or an actual physical item found nearby.
    - RELIGION check — reveals lore/flavor about the location, e.g. "an
      undead presence was here."
    - HISTORY check — reveals lore/flavor about past events at the
      location, e.g. "a conflict happened here."
    - (Other skill-based variants implied but not enumerated — the
      pattern is general: roll which check type fires, then reveal
      something thematically appropriate to that skill.)
- This makes Perception Check less about pure atmosphere and more about
  environmental storytelling/light rewards tied to whichever skill the
  random table lands on — reuses the general "different skills surface
  different flavor" instinct already established elsewhere (Detect Magic
  vs. Detect Good and Evil, dialogue class-alternate-stat checks).
- Not yet decided: full list of which skills can appear on this table,
  exact odds, and the full reward range per skill (only Investigation/
  Religion/History examples given so far).

--- VISITOR (Long/Short Rest event) — design ---
- Should depend on TIME AND PLACE CONTEXT, not be a flat random NPC pull
  — e.g. resting at night on the outskirts of a shady city should surface
  a shady/black-market-type merchant, rather than the same generic
  visitor pool regardless of where/when you rest. This mirrors the
  location-keyed logic already used for Ambush tables.
- NPC ELIGIBILITY: rather than a separate dedicated "traveler" NPC pool,
  ANY existing NPC can be tagged CAN_BE_VISITOR (true/false) to mark them
  as eligible to show up via this event — reuses existing NPCs (traders,
  etc.) instead of authoring a whole new cast just for this system.
- Some Visitors can be QUEST-SPECIFIC, CLASS-SPECIFIC, or ANCESTRY-
  SPECIFIC — i.e. a given visitor might only be eligible to appear for a
  player who has a certain quest flag active, is playing a certain class,
  or is a certain Ancestry/Origin — gated the same way other conditional
  content in Totality already is (DialogueChoiceCondition-style gating).
- SAME TAGGING APPROACH SHOULD APPLY TO AMBUSH TOO (retroactive note): the
  can_be_visitor-style eligibility tag, plus quest/class/ancestry-specific
  gating, should also apply to potential AMBUSHERS, not just Visitors —
  i.e. certain ambushers could be restricted to appear only against
  players with a specific quest flag, class, or ancestry, the same way
  Visitors can be. This generalizes the existing location/narrative-flag-
  keyed Ambush table with an additional class/ancestry/quest-eligibility
  layer.

PRIORITY 2 — SPELL SLOT CONSUMPTION
========================================================
Currently spells can be cast infinitely (no slot deduction wired up yet).
This is the next concrete build step after Rest, since:
  - Multiclass spell slot math is meaningless until slots exist to share.
  - Upcasting (below) requires real per-level slot pools.
  - Rest recovery table above only matters once there's something to
    recover.

Confirmed: SpellSlotComponent already tracks slots PER LEVEL separately
(not a single flat pool) — confirmed by Crown of Stars already existing
as a 7th-level spell. Just needs actual consumption/restoration wired to
the player.

Upcasting rules (once slots exist):
  - Default: casting a spell at a higher slot level than its minimum just
    resolves it normally, no bonus — this is correct for spells with no
    written scaling (e.g. Detect Magic, Charm Person).
  - Only spells that explicitly define scaling get a bonus. Confirmed
    examples: Magic Missile (+1 dart per slot level above 1st), Burning
    Hands / Scorching Ray (extra damage dice per slot level above min).
  - Cantrips are excluded from upcasting entirely (no slot-tier UI for them).
  - Needs an overridable hook per Spell, e.g. getUpcastEffect(slotLevelUsed),
    defaulting to no-op.
  - Slot-tier selection when casting should be a deliberate player choice
    on the cast UI (a slot-tier selector), NOT an automatic "auto-upcast if
    out of matching-level slots" — a player might want to preserve a higher
    slot rather than spend it.

Multiclass slot math:
  - Class levels are ONE SHARED POOL, capped at 30 total across however
    many classes a player invests in (e.g. 30/0/0, or 10/10/10) — this maps
    up through the existing 5:1 conversion to the 150 overall character
    level cap. (Correction from earlier confusion this session: it is NOT
    30 levels independently per class.)
  - For the first 20 levels of class-level investment: follow real D&D 5e
    fractional multiclass slot tables (full casters count full level,
    half-casters count half rounded down, third-casters count a third,
    combined number looked up on the standard multiclass slot table).
  - Levels 21–30 (beyond tabletop's cap): homebrewed by Stefan later, not
    yet defined.
  - Subclass unlock levels should follow the REAL per-class 5e table, not
    a universal number:
      Level 1: Cleric, Sorcerer, Warlock (Otherworldly Patron)
      Level 2: Wizard, Druid
      Level 3: Fighter, Barbarian, Bard, Monk, Paladin, Ranger, Rogue
                Warlock ALSO gets Pact Boon (Chain/Blade/Tome) at level 3
                — this is a SEPARATE choice from Patron (level 1), not the
                same "subclass" moment. Keep as two distinct entries in
                the per-class feature table.
  - Each class level should grant its own discrete feature list (like a
    real 5e class table), generalizing the existing ClassLevelUpRegistry
    pattern used for Wizard's subclass trigger to cover every feature at
    every level, not just subclass picks.

Metamagic (build once base casting/slots work):
  - No action/bonus-action economy in Totality (dropped — hard to balance
    for Minecraft). Metamagic effects that would normally cost a bonus
    action are reinterpreted through the existing cooldown system instead.
  - Quickened Spell = lower cooldown after cast, OR shorter cast/channel
    time before the effect fires — decide per spell which lever applies
    (instant-effect spells like Firebolt only meaningfully support cooldown
    reduction; channeled spells could support either).
  - Buildable now, no dependency: Twinned (retarget to 2nd target),
    Quickened (see above), Extended (double duration), Distant (double
    range), Empowered (reroll damage dice), Heightened (target has
    disadvantage on the save), Careful (allies auto-succeed the save).
  - Blocked on future systems: Subtle Spell (needs Stealth/detection AI to
    mean anything — casting without components is meaningless if nothing
    currently notices spellcasting). Can sit in the registry unimplemented
    until Stealth exists.
  - Needs a CastModifier-style hook between "player initiates cast" and
    "spell resolves" so a Metamagic modifier can rewrite targeting/cost/
    cast-time before the Spell's normal resolution runs. This is a
    retrofit into the existing casting pipeline for all ~13+ implemented
    spells, not just new content.

Detect Magic / School of Magic tagging:
  - Every enchanted item, magical block, and some potions need a School of
    Magic tag (Evocation, Necromancy, Abjuration, Illusion, etc. — schools
    already exist for Spells, need extending to items/blocks).
  - Alchemy potions with multiple effects can carry multiple School tags
    automatically, inherited from whichever effects/ingredients went into
    them — NOT a manual tagging pass.
  - Mob magic detection is split into TWO separate senses, do not conflate:
      - Detect Magic: picks up magical ITEMS/EQUIPMENT a mob is carrying
        (e.g. a Zombie holding a magic sword) — just checks if an
        equipped/held item has a magic/School tag. Not mob-intrinsic.
      - Detect Good and Evil: picks up INHERENTLY magical/otherworldly
        creatures via a CreatureType tag (Fey, Celestial, Fiend, Undead,
        Elemental — the classic 5e list). A Unicorn (Fey) is detected by
        THIS spell, not Detect Magic. Keep these as two independent tags/
        systems, no overlap.
  - Ritual Circle aura (ties into Detect Magic):
      - TWO kinds of circle, structurally different:
          1. Functional circles (built for an ongoing purpose, e.g. a
             permanent Teleport hub) — aura = fixed School tag matching
             the circle's FUNCTION, never fades, not tied to "last spell
             used."
          2. General-purpose ritual circles — aura = School of the LAST
             spell cast there, with a persistence/decay value that scales
             by that spell's power/nature (not a universal timer): a
             mundane spell's trace fades over in-game days/weeks; a
             powerful spell lingers much longer; a reality-bending
             (Wish-tier) spell's trace does not decay at all short of
             actively casting Dispel Magic on it to scrub it early.
      - This is intended as a quest/environmental-storytelling tool (e.g.
        a quest clue: "find the circle that still shows a Necromancy
        trace after all these years").

--- FISHING ---
- Confirmed simple — build EXACTLY like Hypixel Skyblock's fishing system
  (rod tiers, sea-creature drop tables, treasure/junk/fish categorization,
  XP-per-catch scaling with rod/bait). No open design questions.
- Technical injection point already scoped: FishingHookMixin, inject at
  INVOKE_ASSIGN after LootTable.getRandomItems() in FishingHook.retrieve()
  (pattern borrowed from the "Fishing Real" mod).
- Wants a Stardew Valley-style fishing MINIGAME layered on top of the
  Skyblock-style loot/rod/tier system above. CONFIRMED role: the minigame
  gates CATCH SUCCESS specifically — fail the minigame, you don't catch
  the fish at all (the loot roll already happened/is pending, but the
  minigame is the actual catch mechanic, not just a bonus modifier on top
  of an already-guaranteed catch).
  - Fishing MASTERY skills (part of the Fishing skill tree) should make
    the minigame easier/more forgiving — e.g. a bigger catch bar, slower

--- TRADER TIERS + NPC SKILL LEVELS (new system, needs building) ---
- Trader Tiers: not all trading NPCs/villagers offer the same quality of
  deals. Villagers (and other generic traders) should come in tiers, with
  higher tiers offering better prices/stock/rarer items — similar in
  spirit to vanilla villager trade quality but applied more broadly across
  Totality's NPC traders, not just villagers.
- Tier should be visually indicated by COLORING THE NPC'S PROFESSION NAME
  a specific color per tier (e.g. a name-tag or health-bar profession

  color convention already used elsewhere in Totality (tooltips, item
  rarity borders) rather than inventing a new color language.
- Named/unique NPCs (the Banker being the first example) should display

  a glance the same way they'd identify a mob's rank. This likely reuses
  the same HUD label-rendering plumbing already built for MobHealthBarHud
  rather than needing a separate NPC-specific display system.
- NPC Skill Levels: individual NPCs (not just tiers) should have their own
  skill level in relevant trades/services, Skyrim-style — e.g. a given
  blacksmith or enchanter NPC may or may not be able to Identify, Repair,
  or Enchant certain items depending on their own skill level, rather than
  every NPC of a given profession being able to do everything. This means
  NPCs need a skill-level attribute (or a small set of them, per relevant
  service) that gates which services/actions they can actually perform in
  dialogue, separate from and in addition to their overall Trader Tier.
- Open questions not yet resolved: does Trader Tier determine NPC Skill
  Level automatically (i.e. tier is a shorthand/aggregate of skill), or
  are they two fully independent axes (a high-tier trader could still lack
  skill in a specific service, or vice versa)? Not decided this session —
  worth deciding before building, since it changes whether NPCs need one
  stat or several.
- NPC LEVEL/STATS should be able to DRIVE both Trader Tier and Skill
  Level, not just be hand-authored per NPC. Examples given: a level 100
  Enchanter with 400 INT should be a noticeably better enchanter than a
  low-level one; a level 100 Fighter-profession NPC (e.g. a blacksmith or
  weaponsmith) should stock/produce noticeably better-quality items than
  a level 10 one of the same profession. This means NPCs need to run
  through the SAME underlying stat system players do (level, ability
  scores, class where relevant) rather than trade quality being a purely
  hand-set flat value — tier/skill should at least partly be DERIVED from
  an NPC's own level+stats, with hand-authored overrides where a specific
  named NPC (the Banker, a unique quest NPC) needs to be pinned to a
  specific tier regardless of what its stats would otherwise imply.

--- VESTIGES (Critical Role-style evolving legendary items) ---
- Concept confirmed from Critical Role source material: legendary items
  that exist in three states — dormant, awakened, exalted — evolving not
  from raw usage/XP but from narrative/personal-growth milestones (per
  the source material; DM-judged in tabletop).
- Totality implementation approach: each Vestige is tied to its OWN quest.
  The quest's stages ARE the evolution — item is granted DORMANT at some
  point in the quest (sometimes not at the very start — may require
  proving yourself first), a mid-quest turning point flips it to AWAKENED,
  final turn-in delivers EXALTED. No separate "milestone detector" system
  needed — state is just driven by which quest stage/narrative flag is
  currently active. This means state tracking reuses the EXISTING quest/
  NarrativeFlagsComponent system, nothing new to architect there.
- Bespoke per Vestige, matching real Critical Role approach (not a generic
  numeric formula) — Stefan intends to follow how each Vestige actually
  evolved in the Critical Role campaign as the template for that Vestige's
  specific quest.
- Items are typically GIVEN/LOANED by an NPC with an in-fiction reason,
  not found cold in a dungeon (e.g. an in-universe analog of "Fjord loans
  you Dwueth'var because a returning threat means he needs a new
  champion").
- Architecture: VestigeItem interface, implemented alongside the normal
  item class hierarchy. E.g. FenthrasItem extends TotalityBowItem
  implements VestigeItem. A NEW TotalityBowItem base class is needed
  (bows are mechanically distinct enough from melee weapons — draw/charge
  behavior, projectile spawning — to warrant their own base rather than
  extending TotalityWeaponItem). VestigeItem should carry the shared
  dormant/awakened/exalted state machinery (state field driven by the
  owning quest's current stage) plus a way for each concrete Vestige class
  to define its own per-state stat/ability changes.
- Some Vestige quests will eventually require Reputation/Relationship
  and/or Alignment gates once those systems exist (see below) — treat as
  a forward dependency, not a blocker on writing the Vestige system now.

--- ENCHANTING REWORK (Soul Stones — designed earlier, missing from doc until now) ---
- This is the system World Events (Blood Moon etc.) were noted as waiting
  behind on the roadmap (#12) — was designed in an earlier session but
  never made it into this doc until now.
- Confirmed direction: Skyrim-style Soul Stone system, fully REPLACING
  vanilla's enchanting table (not layered alongside it):
    - Soul Stones come in tiers (Petty -> Lesser -> Common -> Greater ->
      Grand, or renamed to fit Totality's lore) — same shape as Skyrim's
      soul gems.
    - Filled via a Soul Trap mechanic/spell — kill a soul-trapped creature,
      the stone captures a soul sized according to the creature's own
      power/tier.
    - Enchanting Table consumes a filled Soul Stone + a weapon/armor piece
      to apply an enchantment.
  - RESOLVED: soul tier maps DIRECTLY to enchantment level as the default
    rule (Petty->I, Lesser->II, Common->III, Greater->IV, Grand->V) —
    handles standard 5-level enchantments (Sharpness, Protection, etc.)
    with no per-enchantment curation needed for the common case.
  - EXCEPTION HANDLING: enchantments with a non-standard number of levels
    (e.g. Unbreaking, which only has 3, not 5) need their own individual
    tier-to-level mapping rather than assuming every enchantment divides
    cleanly across all 5 soul tiers — this is a small curation task
    covering only the exceptions, not every enchantment in the game.
  - MASTERY GATE (new layer): using a given soul tier requires having
    unlocked the corresponding Enchanting mastery perk, independent of
    simply possessing/holding that tier of soul stone — e.g. a "Greater
    Enchanter" mastery perk is required to use Greater souls at all, so a
    player who finds/buys a Greater soul early still cannot apply level
    IV enchantments until that mastery is unlocked. This means Enchanting
    mastery gates CAPABILITY (which soul tiers can be used at all), not
    just potency/output — consistent with how other Totality mastery
    trees (e.g. Alchemy's Knowledge Seeker/Purity) gate capability rather
    than just scaling numbers.
  - OPEN ARCHITECTURAL PROBLEM (flagged, not yet resolved): many vanilla
    enchantments don't actually work correctly against Totality's own
    combat math and need to be REIMPLEMENTED, not just re-tiered. Vanilla
    Sharpness/Protection/etc. work by modifying vanilla attributes
    (ATTACK_DAMAGE, ARMOR, ARMOR_TOUGHNESS) — but Totality's real combat
    resolution runs on its own dice-based system instead (VanillaWeapon-
    Stats' Dice enum, CombatResolver, VanillaDamageInterceptor, AC/d20
    checks via ArmorClass/VanillaArmorStats). This means enchantments
    like Sharpness either do nothing meaningful under Totality's resolver
    (if it ignores the vanilla attribute) or interact incorrectly/double-
    dip (if it doesn't) — this is the SAME category of bug already fixed
    once for vanilla WEAPONS falling through WeaponDataResolver
    incorrectly (see dual-wield/attack-speed fix in the historical
    context), now recurring for ENCHANTMENTS specifically.
  - Likely fix direction (not yet built): enchantment effects that touch
    damage/defense need their own resolver hooks into CombatResolver/
    WeaponDataResolver/ArmorClass directly (e.g. Sharpness adds bonus
    dice or a flat bonus INSIDE the dice-roll calculation, Protection
    reduces damage via whatever mechanism ArmorClass actually uses now)
    rather than relying on vanilla's native attribute-based application.
    Needs a full audit of which vanilla enchantments are combat-math-
    dependent (Sharpness, Protection variants, Fire Aspect, Knockback,
    possibly Smite/Bane of Arthropods as damage-type-conditional bonuses)
    versus which ones are unaffected because they don't touch combat math
    at all (Looting, Silk Touch, Unbreaking, Mending, Fortune) and can
    keep working natively — CONFIRMED this session: these "safe" vanilla
    enchantments stay as-is/unmodified, only the combat-math-dependent
    ones need rework. NOT YET AUDITED OR BUILT — flag as a prerequisite
    piece of work before the Enchanting rework can be considered
    functionally complete, separate from the soul-tier/mastery-gate
    design above (which is resolved).
  - ALSO CONFIRMED: new CUSTOM enchantments (beyond the vanilla list) will
    be added to Totality on top of reworking the existing ones — the
    Enchanting rework isn't just "fix vanilla enchantments," it's also an
    opportunity to add original ones. Not yet designed/listed.
  - EXTENDED SOUL QUALITY AXIS: souls can ALSO carry the existing mob RANK
    system (E through S, the same rank already used for general mob
    difficulty/display) as a quality axis independent of stone TIER
    (Petty/Lesser/Common/Greater/Grand). So a filled soul stone has two
    properties: which TIER of stone it is, and which RANK of creature's
    soul is inside it.
  - NAMED/UNIQUE ARTIFACT SOUL STONES can push enchanting beyond the
    normal vanilla level cap: e.g. a specific hand-crafted artifact soul
    stone (a "Black Star"-style unique item, referencing Skyrim's Azura's
    Star/Black Star artifacts) combined with BOTH the required Enchanting
    mastery AND an S-rank soul could allow reaching enchantment level VI
    — beyond the standard I-V range any normal stone permits. This means
    exceeding the normal cap requires stacking three separate
    requirements (unlocked mastery + a specific named artifact stone +
    the highest soul rank), not just having a big enough generic stone.
    Not yet fully designed (only one example level given — VI via Black-
    Star-equivalent + S-rank soul); other named artifact stones and
    exactly how far beyond VI (if at all) is possible are open.
  - Z-RANK SOULS should do something BETTER still than S-rank — Z rank is
    already reserved elsewhere in Totality for hand-crafted, story-

    rare/hard to obtain by nature. DELIBERATELY DEFERRED this session:
    Stefan wants to design the actual Z-rank soul benefit only once
    Enchanting is built and actually being played/tested, so it can be
    balanced against real in-game numbers rather than designed blind —
    this is a conscious sequencing choice, not an oversight.
  - SOUL TRAP AS AN ABILITY, NOT A SPELL (important scoping decision):
    Soul Trap should be built as an ABILITY rather than a Wizard/caster-
    exclusive spell, specifically so non-caster classes (Fighter,
    Barbarian, Monk, etc.) can also use it — Enchanting shouldn't be
    locked out for martial builds. Mechanic: apply a Soul-Trap-style
    EFFECT/debuff to a target entity (enemy, or an ally too if the player
    is playing an "evil" character); if that entity dies while the effect
    is still active on it, the player automatically captures its soul
    into an empty Soul Stone they're carrying (mirrors Skyrim's Soul Trap
    spell exactly, but generalized as an Ability available mod-wide
    rather than gated to spellcasting classes). Needs: an applied-effect/
    debuff component tracking "soul trap active" per entity, a check on
    entity death for that flag, and logic to consume/fill the first
    empty Soul Stone in the player's inventory on a successful trap+kill.
  - ACQUISITION: Soul Trap should not just be handed out freely — it's
    unlocked via a QUEST, and that quest itself should be LEVEL-GATED
    (requires reaching a certain character level before the quest even
    becomes available to start). LEVEL THRESHOLD: 5 or 10 (not further
    narrowed down this session) — reasoning being that the ability itself
    unlocking early isn't a balance risk on its own, since all the OTHER
    gating factors already layered on top (Enchanting mastery unlocks,
    soul stone tiers/ranks needing to actually be found/earned, the
    mastery-gated access to higher soul tiers above) naturally prevent a
    player from getting overpowered too fast even with early access to
    the ability itself. Consistent with how other capability-granting
    content in Totality is gated behind quests rather than automatic
    unlocks (mirrors the Alchemist's Identify-teaching quest and Mobile
    Banking's dialogue-gated unlock pattern, just with an added level-
    floor on top).

--- TEMPERATURE / THIRST / DISEASE (three related but DISTINCT systems) ---
- CONFIRMED this session: Temperature, Thirst, and (new) Disease will
  each have their OWN distinct effects — NOT a shared/combined
  "environmental exposure" penalty pool. Each needs its own debuff
  profile rather than all three reducing to the same generic penalty.
- DISEASE (new system, motivated by wanting real targets for the Lesser
  Restoration and Greater Restoration spells):
    - Real 5e reference for what these spells actually do, so Disease
      needs enough mechanical substance to give them something to act
      on: LESSER RESTORATION cures one disease OR ends one of a specific
      set of conditions (blinded, deafened, paralyzed, poisoned) affecting
      the target. GREATER RESTORATION handles more severe afflictions —
      reduces/removes Exhaustion levels, restores a reduced ability score,
      removes a curse, restores HP that had been reduced by a draining
      effect, or cures petrification.
    - This means Totality needs an actual DISEASE system (specific named
      diseases, each with its own effect/symptom profile and a way to be
      contracted — e.g. from unclean water tying into Thirst, from
      contaminated food tying into Cooking/Farming, from certain mob
      attacks, from environmental exposure) for Lesser Restoration to
      have something real to cure, rather than the spell existing with
      no disease content to target.
    - NOT YET DESIGNED: the actual list of diseases, their individual
      symptom/debuff profiles, contraction sources, and cure conditions
      beyond "Lesser Restoration cures it." This is a new, real design
      gap on top of Temperature/Thirst — needs its own session.
    - Worth deciding whether Disease is its own HUD-visible bar/indicator
      (like Temperature/Thirst) or a more discrete status-effect-style
      system (diseases show up as named debuffs/conditions rather than a
      depleting resource) — the latter seems more natural given 5e's own
      disease model is condition-based, not resource-based, but not
      confirmed this session.
- THIRST — fully designed this session:
  - BASE DRAIN: bar drains slowly over time by default.
  - FASTER DRAIN SOURCES:
      - Casting spells with the VERBAL component specifically (not all
        spells — only ones requiring speech) drains Thirst a little
        faster per cast; casting many VERBAL spells in a short window
        compounds this (talking dries your mouth out, silent/somatic-only
        spells don't).
      - Being in a desert biome drains Thirst somewhat faster passively.
  - DRINKING WATER — RISK MECHANIC: drinking normal/unpurified water has
    a CHANCE of giving a negative effect alongside restoring Thirst
    (mirrors how eating rotten flesh works in vanilla — you get the
    benefit but risk a debuff). Water from an OCEAN biome specifically
    should have a HIGHER chance of triggering the negative effect than
    other water sources (real-world rationale: seawater/salt water is
    genuinely worse to drink).
  - PURIFIED WATER (new fluid, to be added): a clean water fluid variant
    that presumably avoids or reduces the negative-effect chance from
    drinking normal water — ties into the Engineering/industrial side
    already floated for Thirst (water purification systems).
  - OTHER DRINKS (new items, to be added): additional drinkable items
    beyond plain water that restore Thirst AND grant other effects
    (similar in spirit to Alchemy potions or Cooking dishes) — not yet
    itemized, just confirmed as planned content.
  - DEBUFF TARGETING (important distinction vs. Temperature): Thirst's
    debuffs should primarily affect SPELLCASTING/MANA rather than
    Stamina — rationale given: thirst is mouth/speech-related, so it
    makes more sense to hurt spellcasting (especially VERBAL spells) than
    physical stamina. This is a deliberate split from Temperature, where
    EXTREME HEAT specifically should affect STAMINA instead — the two
    systems intentionally hit different resources so they don't feel
    redundant with each other.
  - REST INTERACTION (resolved): Long Rest does NOT restore Thirst — and
    should actually CAUSE some Thirst loss instead (realistic: you go all
    night without drinking). Short Rest behaves the same way (causes
    loss, does not restore) but the PROBABILITY and AMOUNT of loss differs
    depending on which Short Rest sub-type (the ~25-real-second "30
    minute" version vs. the ~100-real-second "2 hour" version) and
    depending on the specific ACTIVITY performed during that Short Rest
    (exact numbers/activity-specific modifiers not yet decided, just the
    principle that longer/more strenuous Short Rest activities should
    lose more Thirst than shorter/lighter ones).
- TEMPERATURE — fully designed this session:
  - HUD DISPLAY: a state-based indicator (not a numeric bar) using COLOR
    to communicate hot vs. cold — red-tinted for hot, blue-tinted for
    cold (exact visual treatment TBD, but the red/blue hot/cold color
    language is confirmed).
  - END SCORE contributes a SMALL amount to temperature resistance (minor
    factor, not the primary lever).
  - CLOTHING WARMTH is the PRIMARY lever (bigger factor than END): every
    wearable item gets a WARMTH value/stat depending on its type/material
    — e.g. a plain shirt has low Warmth, a wool jacket has high Warmth.
    Explicitly modeled on Skyrim survival mods (Frostfall/Survival Mod,
    Sunhelm) as the reference point for how per-garment warmth values and
    layering should feel.
  - FOOD can ALSO affect Temperature, not just clothing: some foods grant
    Warmth (presumably hot/hearty meals), and some foods can grant
    COLDNESS instead (a cooling effect) — e.g. ice cream specifically
    LOWERS Warmth. This gives Cooking another real mechanical hook
    (alongside the Thirst-offsetting hot/cold meal idea already noted),
    and ties Temperature management partly into what you eat, not just
    what you wear.
  - DEBUFFS (severity/type-dependent, exact tuning deferred to later):
    - HEAT/hot-side debuffs primarily drain STAMINA.
    - COLD/freezing-side debuffs primarily drain HEALTH (more dangerous
      than the heat side, consistent with freezing being the more
      immediately lethal extreme).
    - Exact severity thresholds and drain rates NOT yet decided — "these
      will be thought about later," just the STAMINA-for-heat / HEALTH-
      for-cold split is confirmed now.
  - CROSS-SYSTEM INTERACTION WITH THIRST (confirmed): being in extreme
    HEAT should ALSO increase Thirst drain rate (on top of Thirst's own
    desert-biome drain modifier already designed) — heat and thirst
    reinforcing each other realistically, rather than being fully
    independent systems.

--- CARD-BASED CREATURE COLLECTION (RESOLVED — this IS Bestiary, not a separate system) ---
- QUICK FRAMING: Bestiary is basically Totality's Pokédex — a creature
  compendium with tiered knowledge depth per entry, filled in through

  needing its own separate system. Hunter skill still likely governs the

--- COOKING (reference model + dependency chain) ---
- MECHANIC COMPLEXITY (clarified this session): Cooking is intentionally
  SIMPLE, NOT an Alchemy-style discovery/combination system — no
  ingredient-combining-to-discover-effects mechanic like Alchemy has.
  Basic Cooking should just use straightforward recipes and simple post-
  processing steps, e.g. a Pizza item + a Knife used in a crafting table
  cuts it into slices. This kind of simple transformation/recipe pattern
  is the baseline for most Cooking content, not deep experimentation.
- REFERENCE MODEL: Cooking should be built like "Pam's HarvestCraft"
  (the classic Minecraft food-expansion mod) — many recipes, dedicated
  kitchen utensils/appliances (not just a single cooking pot/stove) —
  but with MORE recipes than Pam's has, i.e. Pam's is the baseline to
  exceed, not just match. This is about RECIPE VOLUME/VARIETY, not
  mechanical depth — the individual recipes themselves stay simple.
- THE INTERESTING PART IS BUTCHERING, NOT COOKING ITSELF: Stefan
  considers Butchering to be where the real mechanical interest in this
  chain comes from, once it's built — Cooking's own recipes are meant to
  stay comparatively basic/simple by design.
- DEPENDENCY CHAIN (important, blocks full Cooking depth): Stefan wants
  to add a lot of Cooking content that depends on BUTCHERING, and
  Butchering itself depends on the CorpseEntity system working for
  ANIMALS, not just humanoid/player death. The existing CorpseEntity
  design (FRESH -> DECOMPOSING -> SKELETAL -> DUST stages, persistent
  inventory, magical items survive all stages, undead reanimation
  chance at SKELETAL) was originally scoped around PLAYER/humanoid
  death flow — it needs to be confirmed/extended to cover animal
  corpses specifically before Butchering can be built, and Butchering
  needs to exist before Cooking's full recipe depth (meat cuts,
  processed animal ingredients, etc.) can be realized. So the real
  build order for Cooking's full vision is: CorpseEntity-for-animals ->
  Butchering system -> expanded Cooking recipes/utensils. Cooking CAN
  still get some basic recipes/utensils built without waiting for this
  whole chain, but won't be "complete" per Stefan's vision until
  Butchering exists.
- Combat stamina regen (Skyrim-style: ~5%/s out of combat, ~2%/s in
  combat, detected via getCombatTracker()) — LIKELY ALREADY BUILT, Stefan
  believes so but wants to verify against the actual code before assuming.
- Timed Block / Perfect Parry — NOT YET BUILT, but confirmed low
  difficulty to add on top of the existing basic Block (V key, dual-
  wield). The only real design piece: a perfectly-timed block should
  STAGGER the opponent for a short duration (exact duration TBD) — this

- Dodge (-5 stamina per dodge, blocked while exhausted) — DESIGNED BUT

--- QUEST XP ECONOMY (character XP sources beyond skill leveling) ---
- Confirmed: character level XP does NOT need to come solely from skill
  level-ups. Quests are a separate, additional character XP source. This
  directly resolves the skill-cap-vs-character-level-cap tension above —
  at a lower skill cap (e.g. 100), skill XP alone wouldn't be enough to
  reach the character level cap, but quest XP can close that gap.
- Quests can ALSO grant SKILL XP directly, not just character XP —
  thematically tied to the quest's subject matter. Confirmed examples:
  an Alchemy-themed quest (e.g. "First Brew") granting Alchemy skill XP;

  standard, reusable reward type any quest's JSON can declare (skill +
  amount), not a one-off per quest.
- Quests can ALSO grant TEMPORARY skill XP boosts as a reward, not just
  a one-time XP lump sum — e.g. "double skill XP for 10 minutes" as a
  timed buff reward. This is a different reward type from flat skill XP
  and should be modeled as its own timed-modifier reward (reuses the same
  skillImproveMult modifier-stack architecture already designed for
  potions/enchantments that boost skill leveling speed — see the
  standing-stone-equivalent modifier system already sketched for skills).

- Simple fetch/kill daily types (the bulk/volume content, distinct from
  the bespoke Headhunting bounties above): straightforward "bring NPC-X
  N of item-Y" (e.g. bring the Blacksmith 10 Iron, bring the Alchemist 20
  Blue Mountain Flowers) or "kill N of mob-type" (e.g. kill 10 Zombies for
  a Priest) quests. These need NO new mechanics — just the existing
  data-driven quest template system with an item/mob target and a count,
  tied to whichever NPC makes thematic sense for that resource (Blacksmith
  = ores/metals, Alchemist = ingredients, Priest = undead, etc.). This is
  the low-effort, high-volume quest type that fills out the board day to
  day, with Headhunting-style bounties as the occasional bespoke/higher-
  effort content on the same board.

--- DAILY QUESTS + BULLETIN BOARD (new system, needs building) ---
- Wants a Solo Leveling-style daily quest system: quests that reset on a
  daily cycle, presumably scaling with player/class level, giving
  reliable repeatable progression/rewards.
- Delivery mechanism: a physical BULLETIN BOARD / notice board block the
  player interacts with to see available quest postings and pick one up
  — reference is the "Missives" mod for Skyrim (procedurally-generated,
  low-stakes fetch/kill/delivery-style quests posted on a board, no NPC
  dialogue required to receive them, unlike Totality's normal hand-
  authored dialogue-driven quests).
- This gives Totality two parallel quest delivery paths: hand-authored,
  narrative, dialogue-gated quests (Mobile Banking, First Brew, Vestige
  quests, etc.) vs. lightweight, repeatable, board-posted daily quests
  that don't need bespoke NPC dialogue per quest.
- Open questions, not yet decided: how many quests appear on the board at
  once and how are they chosen/generated (fully random from a pool per
  category? weighted toward skills the player is actively using? tied to
  the quest's existing reset_flags/objective-group tech already built for
  other quests?); do daily quests reuse the exact same underlying quest-
  template JSON system as regular quests (objectives, turn-in, rewards)
  just with a daily-reset flag and no dialogue requirement, or do they
  need a lighter-weight parallel data format since they're meant to be
  numerous/low-effort to author (likely procedurally-assembled from
  templates rather than hand-written one at a time, given the volume a
  "daily quest" system implies)? Given Totality's quest system is already
  data-driven and generic, reusing the same template format with a
  daily-reset+board-sourced flag seems like the natural fit, but not
  explicitly confirmed this session.

--- HEADHUNTING-STYLE BOUNTIES (Skyrim "Headhunter" mod reference) ---
- Reference mod confirmed (Headhunter - Bounties Redone, Skyrim): bounty
  quests where killing the target isn't enough on its own — you must
  loot a TROPHY item from the corpse (a head/signet/marked item) and
  bring it back as PROOF. Turning in without the trophy is explicitly
  rejected by the quest-giver ("you expect me to believe that?").
- Also includes a NON-LETHAL capture path: target can be made to kneel/
  surrender at low health, then a dialogue option takes them prisoner
  instead of killing them (handed to an authority figure rather than
  looted), and separately some bounty types can be resolved via
  Persuasion/Intimidation alone (e.g. intimidating a thief into returning
  stolen goods) with no combat at all.
- Delivered via the same Notice Board pattern as Missives — confirms this
  should be UNIFIED with the Daily Quests/Bulletin Board system above,
  not built as a third separate quest-delivery mechanism. A bounty board
  posting could just be one quest TYPE among the board's daily offerings
  (fetch/kill/delivery/bounty), all going through the same board UI.
- Totality-specific fit: this slots naturally onto systems already built:
    - Bounty targets = boss-tier mobs, likely tied to the existing
      Rank system (E-S, hand-crafted Z for named/story bosses) — a
      board-posted bounty could target a randomly-rolled higher-rank mob
      in a specific structure/biome, similar to how Dimensional Gates
      already spawn special mobs.
    - Trophy-drop-on-kill is a simple, generic loot-table addition (target
      mob type gets a guaranteed "proof" item drop on death) — no new
      engine capability needed, just a quest-linked loot entry.
    - Non-lethal capture (kneel-at-low-health -> dialogue-surrender ->
      hand off) reuses the existing Dialogue system plus presumably a
      "kneeling/surrendered" AI state on the mob — worth checking if this
      is close enough to the Dominate Person "present a menu of actions"
      pattern to share some plumbing, or if it needs its own surrender-
      state AI flag.
    - Persuasion/Intimidation-resolved bounties (no combat at all) reuse
      existing DialogueChoiceCondition roll-based checks (same pattern as
      the Banker's account-opening alternate paths) applied to a bounty
      context instead of a banking one.
- CORRECTION on delivery structure: the Notice Board is actually an
  OPTIONAL ADD-ON in the reference mod (only relevant if you also have a
  Missives-style notice-board mod installed) — the Headhunter mod's
  NORMAL/default delivery is via an NPC, specifically a Jarl's steward-
  type figure (e.g. Proventus Avenicci equivalent), not a board. So for
  Totality: the PRIMARY bounty-giver should be a proper dialogue NPC
  (a steward/quest-giver figure, following the same pattern as the
  Banker/Alchemist), with the Bulletin Board being an ADDITIONAL,
  secondary delivery channel layered on top (for daily/repeatable/no-
  dialogue-needed bounties specifically) rather than the sole or primary
  way to receive bounties.
- Turn-in without a trophy — TWO separate roll-based alternate paths, not
  one:
    1. PERSUASION path (you actually did kill the target, you just don't
       have the trophy — lost it, whatever): a straightforward Persuasion
       roll convinces the commissioner to believe/pay out anyway.
    2. DECEPTION path (you did NOT actually kill the target, lying about
       it): also roll-based, but the DIFFICULTY SCALES WITH ELAPSED TIME
       since the quest was accepted. Claiming a kill mere seconds after
       accepting the quest should be an extremely high difficulty (e.g.
       ~99), passable in practice only via a natural 20 or a very high
       relevant modifier/level; claiming it after a plausible amount of
       time has passed should use a normal/lower difficulty. This means
       the Deception check needs to read quest-acceptance timestamp vs.
       current time as an input to the roll's DC, not just a flat
       difficulty — a genuinely novel mechanic, worth building as a
       reusable "time-since-X-affects-DC" pattern in case other systems
       want the same trick later (lying about anything time-sensitive).
- Non-lethal surrender path also gets multiple roll options, not just one
  fixed check:
    - Standard Persuasion roll to talk the target into surrendering.
    - Class/build-specific alternate stat substitution — e.g. a Barbarian
      can use an INTIMIDATION check keyed off STRENGTH instead of the
      normal governing stat, reflecting a physically-imposing character
      intimidating through raw presence rather than social skill. This
      generalizes into a broader rule worth writing down once: certain
      classes/builds should be able to substitute their signature ability
      score into checks that would normally key off a different stat
      (mirrors 5e's occasional "you can use X instead of Y for this
      check" exceptions) — likely relevant well beyond just this one
      bounty interaction, so worth building as a general
      DialogueChoiceCondition capability (declare an alternate governing
      stat per class/condition) rather than a one-off for Barbarians.
- Not yet decided: does a captured/surrendered bounty target go anywhere
  meaningful in Totality (an actual prison/holding system, like the
  Skyrim mod's "prisoner stays in prison permanently"), or is capture
  purely a flavor/reward-flavor choice with no persistent world-state
  consequence? Worth deciding before building the surrender path, since
  a real prison system is a bigger commitment than just branching
  dialogue.

--- CRIME SYSTEM (new, Skyrim-inspired but corrected) ---
- Confirmed this session: this should be its OWN real reputation-adjacent
  system (a "you committed a crime, the world reacts" mechanic), Skyrim-
  inspired but with deliberate improvements over Skyrim's actual
  implementation.
- KEY CORRECTION vs. Skyrim: witness detection must be SENTIENCE-GATED.
  Skyrim is (in)famous for non-sentient/implausible witnesses being able
  to somehow report crimes to guards (a chicken, a horse). Totality's
  version should only let ACTUALLY SENTIENT/CAPABLE witnesses report a
  crime — a normal horse should NOT be able to tell a guard you stole
  something, UNLESS that horse is specifically magical/sentient (e.g. a
  talking/intelligent magical creature, tying into the existing Speak
  With Animals-adjacent thinking and general creature-intelligence
  tagging elsewhere in the mod). This likely needs a WITNESS-CAPABLE flag
  on entities (sentient NPCs = yes by default, ordinary animals = no by
  default, specific magical/named creatures = yes as an exception),
  rather than treating "was nearby" as sufficient to generate a witness
  report the way vanilla Skyrim effectively does.
- Beyond the witness-sentience fix, NOT yet designed in detail: how
  bounty/crime severity accrues, how guards/authority figures react at
  different severity thresholds, whether this integrates with the
  existing Relationship system (a witnessed crime presumably should be
  able to sour Relationship with witnesses/victims, similar to how the
  Caleb/Fjord narrative-flag example already establishes that specific
  story actions can override relationship independent of tier).
- BUILD ORDER FOR THIS SYSTEM (confirmed this session): first make an
  actual LIST OF CRIMES, and crucially, crimes are DIMENSION-SPECIFIC —
  what counts as a crime depends on where/whose territory you're in. E.g.
  in the Nether, a Piglin witnessing you mine Gold counts as a crime to
  Piglins specifically (references vanilla Piglin gold-aggro lore), even
  though mining gold is obviously not a crime in the Overworld. This means
  the crime list needs a location/faction-scoping field per crime type,
  not one universal crime list.
- PRISON STRUCTURE(S) — needs at least one, likely SEVERAL, physical
  prison structures depending on crime type/severity:
    - Standard crimes (theft, minor violence, etc.) → a normal, mundane
      prison structure (Skyrim-style holding cell/jail).
    - REALITY-BENDING crimes (using Wish-tier magic destructively, other
      cosmic-scale transgressions) → could trigger a UNIVERSAL-level
      response: a cosmic/interdimensional AGENCY intervenes and sends the
      offender to a completely different kind of prison (a "space
      prison" or similar) — a much higher-stakes, higher-narrative-weight
      consequence than a normal jail cell. This ties thematically into
      the Wish/reality-bending spell list already discussed and hints at
      an eventual watcher/enforcer faction concept (not yet named or
      designed).
  Not yet decided: exact number/tiers of prison structures needed, what
  escape/sentence mechanics look like, or how a player actually ends up
  IN one (auto-teleport on conviction? an escort quest? combat-then-
  capture like the Bounty surrender path?).
