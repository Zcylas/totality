TOPIC 1 — CHARACTER CREATION: ATTRIBUTE GENERATION
================================================================================
(Part 1 of an ongoing attribute-balance redesign. Part 2, covering
post-creation/leveling attribute scaling — and the actual bounded-
accuracy fix this was originally motivated by — is a separate future
topic, not yet added to this document. See Topic 1's Section 3.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.0 THE PROBLEM THIS IS FIXING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Totality's existing character system has ALL 8 ability scores (STR,
DEX, CON, END, INT, WIS, CHA, FTH) start at 10 (modifier 0). CORRECTION
(discovered later in this same design pass, see Topic 2 for the full
follow-up): the actual existing level-up rate is 5 FLAT attribute
points per player level, not 1 as originally believed when this
section was first written — and the level cap has since been raised
to 150 (from the 100 referenced when Topic 1 began). At the old rate,
that's up to 750 total points if dumped into one stat, which is far
beyond even the +45-50 modifier problem described below — see Topic 2
for the corrected math and cadence options actually being considered
now.

This breaks D&D's bounded-accuracy assumption at high level: a +45 to
+50 modifier on an attack roll makes AC essentially meaningless (d20 +
45 vs. any realistic AC value almost never misses). This was flagged
in an earlier design discussion, which explored several fixes (reduce
points/level, hard-cap attributes, decouple attributes from D&D
modifiers entirely via sqrt-scaling, replace AC with a Defense-Rating/
hit-chance formula) without settling on one. THIS TOPIC starts fixing
the problem from a different angle: how attributes are GENERATED at
character creation, borrowing directly from D&D 5e's own actual tools
for this (point buy's progressive cost curve, which is specifically
designed to prevent runaway single-stat dumping).

A future topic will need to revisit the LEVEL-UP side of this —
whether attribute points awarded per level continue at 1/level flat,
get reduced, get their own progressive cost curve matching character
creation, or something else entirely — since fixing character-
creation generation alone does NOT fully solve the level-100-reaches-
110-STR problem if level-up points remain uncapped/flat.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 CHARACTER CREATION — TWO METHODS ONLY (NOT THREE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Real D&D 5e offers three official ability-score generation methods:
Point Buy, Standard Array, and Rolling. Totality uses ONLY TWO of
these — Standard Array is explicitly EXCLUDED (a fixed pre-generated
set doesn't fit well with 8 attributes instead of 6, and doesn't offer
anything Point Buy doesn't already cover better).

  Method 1 — Point Buy:
    Base score: 8 (not 10 — this is a deliberate divergence from
    Totality's existing base-10 convention, specifically for the
    character-creation method; see Section 1.4's open item about
    reconciling this with the rest of the system).

    Cap before origin/species bonus: 15 (same as real D&D 5e).

    Progressive cost table (UNCHANGED from real D&D 5e — the table
    itself doesn't need to scale with stat COUNT, only the total pool
    does, see below):
      Score | Cost
      8     | 0
      9     | 1
      10    | 2
      11    | 3
      12    | 4
      13    | 5
      14    | 7
      15    | 9

    Total point pool: 36 (SCALED from D&D 5e's 27 points across 6
    stats). Reasoning: D&D's 27 points ÷ 6 stats = 4.5 points/stat on
    average (the Standard Array 15/14/13/12/10/8 costs exactly 27 and
    averages 4.5/stat too, confirming this is the intended per-stat
    budget, not an arbitrary total). Scaled to Totality's 8 stats:
    4.5 × 8 = 36. This preserves the SAME per-stat budget/generosity
    as real D&D's point buy, just extended across 2 more attributes
    (END, FTH), rather than arbitrarily picking a bigger number.

    The progressive cost curve is the actual mechanism worth keeping —
    it's cheap to bring a weak stat up to "not terrible" but
    increasingly expensive to push any single stat toward the
    ceiling, which is exactly the kind of restraint the flat-point
    system lacked.

  Method 2 — Critical Role-style rolling (Matthew Mercer's house
  rule, confirmed via research, not just standard 4d6-drop-lowest):
    Mechanic: roll 4d6, drop the lowest die, ONCE PER ATTRIBUTE (8
    times total for Totality's 8 stats — scaled up from the
    original's 6 rolls). Sum the total across all 8 results. Assign
    the 8 numbers to whichever attributes the player wants.

    REROLL FLOOR (the actual homebrew addition, confirmed via
    Matthew Mercer's own stated rule — not part of standard/vanilla
    4d6-drop-lowest, which has no safety net at all): if the total of
    all generated numbers falls below a threshold, the player may
    reroll the ENTIRE set from scratch.

    Original Critical Role threshold: 70, across 6 stats (≈11.67/stat
    average floor — notably BELOW true 4d6-drop-lowest's actual
    average of ~12.24/stat, since the point is to catch genuinely
    unlucky results, not to guarantee an above-average outcome).

    SCALED for Totality's 8 stats (same proportional-scaling logic as
    Point Buy's pool above): 11.67/stat × 8 stats ≈ 93. Locked in as
    the starting reroll threshold: 93.

    This method skews higher on average than Point Buy (true 4d6-
    drop-lowest averages ~12.24/stat vs. Point Buy's 4.5-points-of-
    budget baseline) while still preserving randomness/excitement —
    the floor just prevents a player from being crippled by bad luck
    the way vanilla rolling can.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 ORDER OF OPERATIONS — SPECIES/ORIGIN FIRST, THEN ATTRIBUTES, BONUS
    APPLIED LAST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONFIRMED sequence (matches real D&D 5e's actual official order, not
just "race first" or "points first" — the two are interleaved):

  1. Choose species/origin FIRST (Totality's existing
     SpeciesSelectionScreen -> OriginSelectionScreen flow) — the
     player now KNOWS what bonus is coming (e.g. a species/origin that
     grants +2 STR).
  2. Assign base attribute scores SECOND, via EITHER Point Buy OR the
     Critical-Role-style roll (Section 1.1) — the player can optimize
     their spending/assignment KNOWING the bonus that's about to
     apply (e.g. only buying STR up to 13 if they know +2 is coming,
     saving points for elsewhere, rather than buying all the way to
     15 themselves and having the bonus push past the intended
     ceiling).
  3. Apply the species/origin bonus (Totality's existing
     `originBonus` layer in PlayerStats) ON TOP of whatever was
     assigned in step 2, LAST — at the existing ConfirmAncestryScreen
     step.

  This is why the sequence matters: species-first-but-bonus-applied-
  last is what makes point buy (or the roll method) actually SYNERGIZE
  with species/origin choice, instead of the two systems just sitting
  side by side ignoring each other.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3 WHAT THIS TOPIC DOES NOT YET COVER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This topic only covers ATTRIBUTE GENERATION AT CHARACTER CREATION. It
does NOT yet address:
  - Whether/how attribute points awarded PER LEVEL (actually 5 flat
    points/level currently, at a now-150 level cap — see Topic 2,
    which covers this in full) change as a result of this redesign.
  - Whether the progressive-cost-curve PHILOSOPHY from Point Buy
    should also apply to level-up point spending — RESOLVED in Topic
    2: no, level-up points stay flat/simple by decision, with pool
    size and an optional Feat-or-stats choice as the actual balance
    levers instead.
  - The underlying attack-roll-vs-AC bounded-accuracy problem this
    whole redesign was originally motivated by (Section 1.0) — this
    needs its own future topic in this document to actually close the
    loop.

TOPIC 2 — LEVEL-UP ATTRIBUTE POINT CADENCE (Part 2 of the attribute
   redesign flagged as pending in Topic 1, Section 1.3)
================================================================================
STATUS: UNDECIDED — this topic documents the analysis/options, not a
locked decision. Cadence is explicitly not yet chosen; being handed to
ChatGPT for further review before finalizing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.0 CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The existing level-up system grants 5 flat attribute points PER LEVEL.
With the level cap now at 150 (raised from the 100 referenced in
Topic 1), that's up to 750 total points if dumped into one stat — far
beyond even the original +45-50 modifier problem that motivated Topic
1. This topic works out safer cadences.

DECIDED (this pass): level-up points stay FLAT/SIMPLE — no progressive
cost curve applied to level-up spending (unlike Point Buy's curve at
character creation, Topic 1.1). The only lever available to control
growth is POOL SIZE (how many points total) and/or an opportunity-cost
mechanic (a Feat-or-stats choice, D&D-style).

Real D&D 5e reference point (confirmed via research): ASIs (Ability
Score Improvements) arrive at levels 4, 8, 12, 16, 19 for most classes
(5 total over a 20-level campaign; Fighter gets 2 extra at 6/14 = 7
total, Rogue gets 1 extra at 10 = 6 total). Each ASI is EITHER +2 to
one score, OR +1 to two different scores, OR a Feat instead (no stat
increase, a unique ability/feature instead). Ability scores CANNOT
exceed 20 via ASI (rare magic-item exceptions aside). The important
insight: D&D's ASI schedule is only safe BECAUSE it's paired with that
hard 20 cap — the schedule alone isn't what bounds power.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 THREE CADENCE OPTIONS COMPARED (level cap 150)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Target reference: keep a fully-dumped single stat's LEVEL-UP
contribution to its modifier somewhere around +20 (rather than the
old +45-50), so a maxed character is powerful but AC/bounded-accuracy
still means something.

  Option A — every 5 levels, 1 flat point:
    150 / 5 = 30 opportunities -> 30 points total if all dumped into
    one stat. Starting from Point Buy's ~15-19 post-origin-bonus cap
    (Topic 1), a fully dumped stat lands around 45-49, giving a
    modifier around +17.5 to +19.5 — LANDS IN THE TARGET RANGE. This
    option is safe ON ITS OWN, without needing anything else bolted
    on (no curve, no hard cap, no Feat trade-off required).

  Option B — every 5 levels, 2 flat points:
    30 opportunities -> 60 points total if all dumped. Stat reaches
    ~75-79, modifier around +32.5 to +34.5 — BREAKS bounded accuracy
    again, same category of problem as the original 5/level system,
    just less severe.

  Option C — D&D-style, every 4 levels, choose Feat OR attribute
  points (+2/one stat or +1/two stats per instance, matching real
  ASI's split):
    150 / 4 ≈ 37 opportunities -> UP TO 74 points if every single
    opportunity is spent on stats with none going to Feats. Stat
    reaches ~93, modifier around +41.5 in the WORST CASE — also
    breaks bounded accuracy, UNLESS players are actually taking Feats
    a meaningful fraction of the time.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 THE KEY PROBLEM WITH OPTION C (D&D-style): the Feat safety valve
    is weaker than it first appears
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Option C's safety depends entirely on players choosing Feats often
enough to meaningfully reduce total stat-point accumulation. This is
an OPPORTUNITY COST, not a hard prevention — a player who ignores
Feats entirely and dumps every single opportunity into one stat hits
the same runaway problem the old flat system had (worst case above).

THIS GETS WEAKER STILL once Stefan's follow-up point is factored in:
SOME FEATS ALSO GRANT ATTRIBUTE BONUSES (matches real D&D's "half-
feat" pattern — e.g. Resilient gives +1 to a stat PLUS a saving-throw
proficiency, rather than being a pure non-stat choice). If Totality's
Feats include similar half-feats, then "choosing a Feat instead of
stats" doesn't fully divert from stat growth in every case — a player
who always picks half-feats-that-grant-a-stat-point still accumulates
significant stat growth while technically "not taking the ASI option."
This means Option C's actual safety margin is somewhere BETWEEN the
worst case above (74 points, all-stats) and a fully Feat-diverted
best case — the exact number depends on how many of Totality's future
Feats end up being half-feats vs. pure non-stat Feats, which isn't
decided yet (Feats themselves aren't designed in this document).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 CURRENT LEAN (not locked) + WHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Option A is the only one of the three that's safe ON ITS OWN, without
depending on player behavior (Feat-taking frequency) or additional
mechanisms (a cost curve, a hard cap) that aren't decided yet. Options
B and C both reopen the original bounded-accuracy problem unless
paired with something else — a per-stat cap (D&D's 20-equivalent), a
progressive cost curve (explicitly rejected for level-up spending in
2.0), or a real constraint on how many Feats can be half-feats.

This is NOT a final decision — flagged for ChatGPT review alongside
the rest of the balance/RPG documents before anything gets locked in.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 OPEN ITEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Cadence itself (Option A/B/C or a variant) — UNDECIDED.
  - Whether Totality's Feats will include half-feats (feats that also
    grant a small attribute bonus, D&D-style) — not decided, Feats
    aren't designed yet. This directly affects Option C's real safety
    margin (Section 2.2) and should be resolved before Option C is
    seriously considered.
  - Whether a per-stat hard cap (a Totality-scaled equivalent of D&D's
    20) should exist regardless of which cadence is picked, as a
    backstop independent of point-accumulation math.
  - The +20-ish target modifier range (Section 2.1) is a reasoned
    starting point, not a locked balance target — revisit once actual
    combat-math/AC-scaling (referenced in Topic 1, Section 1.0) is
    designed.
