TOTALITY — SHARED CROSS-SYSTEM FOUNDATIONS
(Material/Substance/Form, Chemistry integration boundary, generic
 Process Job lifecycle, Exposure/Hazard, container compatibility,
 phase transitions, pollution hooks, machine condition hooks,
 knowledge/unlock separation)

STATUS: current working specification. This is the BRIDGE document
between Food Ecosystem, Disease/Spell, RPG, Industrial, Chemistry,
Construction, Smithing, and any future system — it exists specifically
so those systems don't each reinvent the same handful of cross-cutting
concepts independently. Sections here are deliberately CONCISE
ownership boundaries and future extension points, NOT full
implementations — the full designs for Chemistry, Industrial
machinery, exposure/RPG resistance mechanics, etc. remain separate,
future work. Do not read this document as a substitute for those.

Material in this document is ADOPTED (as a boundary/contract) unless
marked STUB, TBD, PLACEHOLDER, SUGGESTED, or PLANNED — same convention
as every other Totality design document.

Companion documents this bridges:
  TOTALITY_FOOD_ECOSYSTEM_CURRENT.txt
  TOTALITY_DISEASE_SPELL_INTERVENTION_DESIGN.txt
  TOTALITY_CHEMISTRY_DESIGN.txt        (future — full Chemistry design)
  TOTALITY_INDUSTRIAL_DESIGN.txt       (existing/future)
  TOTALITY_RPG_DESIGN.txt              (existing/future)

1. MATERIAL, SUBSTANCE, AND FORM
================================================================================
Three distinct concepts, deliberately kept separate rather than
collapsed into one "material" idea:

  SUBSTANCE — chemical identity. Iron, Water, Sodium Chloride, Ethanol.
    This is WHAT something fundamentally is at the chemistry level.

  MATERIAL — an authored PRACTICAL material built from one or more
    substances for gameplay purposes. Steel, Concrete, Leather, Glass,
    Wood. A material is a game-design-facing concept; it may be
    chemically simple (mostly one substance) or a genuine mixture
    (Steel = Iron + Carbon + other alloying substances), but either
    way it's authored as a coherent, usable THING, not derived
    on-the-fly from raw chemistry every time it's referenced.

  FORM — how a material/substance currently EXISTS in gameplay. Ingot,
    Dust, Plate, Block, Liquid, Gas, Molten, Slurry, Crystal, Solution.
    The SAME material can exist in multiple forms (Iron as Ingot, Dust,
    Molten, or a Block), and the same FORM concept is shared across
    domains (Fluid Material's FluidPhase, Section 9 of the Food
    Ecosystem doc, IS a form-level concept — Liquid/Gas/Molten/Slurry
    map directly onto this shared vocabulary rather than being a
    Food-specific invention).

  public record MaterialDefinition(
          Identifier id, Identifier substanceId,   -- primary
                                                        chemical
                                                        identity, if
                                                        singular/
                                                        simple
          Map<Identifier, Float> substanceComposition,  -- for
                                                             genuine
                                                             mixtures/
                                                             alloys
          Set<Identifier> availableForms
  ) {}

  Items, blocks, fluids, and other in-game objects map to
  material/substance/form through STATIC REGISTRIES — an Iron Ingot
  item is registered as (material=totality:iron, form=INGOT); Molten
  Iron fluid content is registered as (material=totality:iron,
  form=MOLTEN). This mapping is DATA, resolved once at registration,
  not computed per-instance.

  RULE: ordinary game objects do NOT carry a dynamic composition
  component. An Iron Ingot doesn't need per-stack chemistry data — its
  composition is exactly whatever `totality:iron`'s
  MaterialDefinition says, looked up generically. DYNAMIC per-stack
  composition (Section 3 below) is reserved specifically for
  laboratory-created samples, mixtures, impure/unknown substances, and
  similar cases where the actual composition genuinely varies
  instance-to-instance — see Section 3 for exactly where that line
  sits.

  This foundation is explicitly SHARED by: Chemistry, Fluid Material
  (Food Ecosystem doc Section 9), Smithing, Construction, Processing
  (Food Ecosystem doc Section 7), Industrial manufacturing, Equipment,
  Economy, and Codex. None of those systems should invent their own
  competing material/substance/form vocabulary.

2. CHEMISTRY INTEGRATION BOUNDARY
================================================================================
Chemistry itself is NOT designed in this document (that's
TOTALITY_CHEMISTRY_DESIGN.txt, future work) — only its ownership
boundary against everything else is established here, so other
systems know what to expect from it without needing its full design
yet.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2a. Ownership split
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CHEMISTRY owns: substances, composition, analysis, reactions,
    concentration, catalysts, and chemical research.
  PROCESSING owns: generic transactional transformation EXECUTION
    (the actual "consume inputs, produce outputs, over time, with a
    station capability" engine — Food Ecosystem doc Section 7).
    Chemistry reactions likely RUN THROUGH Processing's execution
    engine rather than reimplementing their own.
  FLUID MATERIAL owns: storage and movement of Liquid/Gas/Molten/
    Slurry forms (Food Ecosystem doc Section 9). A chemical solution
    IS a fluid, storage-wise — Chemistry doesn't reinvent tanks/pipes.
  MACHINE/ENERGY owns: containment, throughput, efficiency, pressure,
    temperature control, and supplied power for whatever apparatus a
    reaction happens in.
  PRODUCT SAFETY owns: hazards (Food Ecosystem doc Section 6b/6b-2's
    SafetyProfile/hazards model) — a dangerous chemical result is
    expressed through the SAME hazard system everything else uses, not
    a Chemistry-specific danger flag.
  EXPOSURE (Section 4 below) owns: how a hazard actually REACHES a
    creature (route, dose, duration) — Chemistry produces hazardous
    substances, Exposure resolves what happens when something is
    actually exposed to them.
  ENVIRONMENT receives: spills, emissions, and pollution as inputs to
    its existing EnvironmentContext (Food Ecosystem doc Section 5) —
    Chemistry doesn't maintain a parallel environmental-state system.
  CODEX records: chemical discoveries, through the same typed
    DiscoveryEvent/DiscoveryFact pattern (Food Ecosystem doc Section
    4c) every other domain already uses.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2b. Chemistry vs. Alchemy — kept SEPARATE, not merged
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CHEMISTRY is repeatable/measurable — same inputs, same conditions,
    same result, every time, governed by consistent in-world rules.
  ALCHEMY uses magical reagent properties and supernatural rules —
    results can depend on things Chemistry doesn't (reagent "quality"
    in a magical sense, caster skill/attunement, lunar/dimensional
    conditions, etc.) and aren't necessarily reproducible the same way.

  These stay TWO SEPARATE APIs. Do not merge them, even though they
  might share some underlying infrastructure (both could reasonably
  use the Fluid Material API for liquid reagents, for instance).
  EXPLICITLY LEAVE ROOM for a future "Arcane Chemistry" hybrid concept
  WITHOUT requiring Chemistry and Alchemy themselves to merge — Arcane
  Chemistry, if it ever exists, would be its own bridge/hybrid system
  reading from both, not a fusion of the two base APIs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2c. Decomposition is not automatically reversible
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  RULE: Chemistry's decomposition/breakdown mechanics must NOT imply
  perfect reversibility (breaking something down and reassembling it
  doesn't guarantee getting the exact original back) or automatic
  synthesis of ARBITRARY items from raw components (Chemistry isn't a
  universal matter-replicator — what can be synthesized is whatever's
  been explicitly authored as a valid reaction/recipe, not "anything
  the player can imagine from the right raw substances").

3. STATIC AND DYNAMIC CHEMICAL COMPOSITION
================================================================================
Directly extends Section 1's material/substance/form split into the
specific static-vs-dynamic data question.

  STATIC — `MaterialCompositionDefinition`, for ORDINARY items/
    materials (Section 1's MaterialDefinition.substanceComposition IS
    this — every Iron Ingot has IDENTICAL composition, looked up from
    one shared definition, never stored per-stack).

  DYNAMIC — `ChemicalSampleComponent`, ONLY for:
    - mixtures (a player-combined blend of several substances)
    - custom solutions (a specific concentration a player mixed,
      distinct from an "official" pre-defined solution)
    - impure samples (something that started pure but picked up
      contamination — ties into the Contaminants axis already
      established for fluids, Food Ecosystem doc Section 9a's
      composition-vs-contaminants split)
    - unknown substances (something the player hasn't identified yet
      — ties into Codex discovery, Section 8 below)
    - experimental products (a novel combination without an
      established MaterialDefinition yet)

  public record ChemicalSampleComponent(
          Map<Identifier, Float> composition,   -- same normalized-
                                                    fraction
                                                    convention as
                                                    Fluid Material's
                                                    composition/
                                                    contaminants axes
          SafetyProfile safety, Optional<Identifier> knownIdentityId
                                    -- populated once/if the sample
                                       has been identified as a known
                                       substance; absent while unknown
  ) {}

  RULE: dynamic composition must be QUANTIZED sufficiently to preserve
  practical stacking — same principle already established for fluid
  contaminants (Food Ecosystem doc Section 9b-2's quantization fix):
  two samples with near-identical but not bit-for-bit-equal
  composition should still be able to stack, via the same
  StackMergePolicyRegistry/StackMergeService mechanism (Food Ecosystem
  doc Section 6d) rather than a Chemistry-specific reinvention of that
  problem.

4. GENERIC PROCESS JOB LIFECYCLE
================================================================================
Multiple systems (Processing, Cooking, Chemistry, Industrial machines)
each need a "thing is being worked on over time, with inputs
committed, that can be cancelled/interrupted/automated" lifecycle. This
should be ONE shared infrastructure, not four separate reinventions.

  public record ProcessJobComponent(
          UUID jobId, Identifier jobType,   -- e.g. totality:cooking,
                                                totality:chemistry_reaction,
                                                totality:smithing,
                                                totality:industrial_craft
          UUID ownerId, List<ReservedInput> reservedInputs,
          int currentStage, List<StageResult> completedStages,
          JobLifecycle lifecycle, long readyAtTick,
          Optional<Identifier> automationSourceId
                             -- present if an automated system (not a
                                player) initiated this job; absent for
                                player-initiated jobs. Ties into the
                                existing "automation generally doesn't
                                grant XP/discoveries" default
                                (Food Ecosystem doc Section 0)
  ) {}

  public enum JobLifecycle { ACTIVE, PROCESSING, WAITING, READY,
      COMPLETED, CANCELLED }

  This is deliberately the SAME SHAPE as Cooking's `CookingBatchComponent
  `/`BatchLifecycle` (Food Ecosystem doc Section 17k) — that Cooking-
  specific type should be understood as one CONCRETE APPLICATION of
  this generic Process Job pattern, not a coincidentally-similar
  parallel invention. Processing, Chemistry, and Industrial machines
  should all build on this SAME shared lifecycle infrastructure:

  Shared behaviors every domain reuses rather than reimplementing:
    - input RESERVATION at job start (transactional, per the Food doc's
      global conventions)
    - STAGE progression tracking
    - PERSISTENCE (the job survives as data independent of any
      specific station/machine, same "batch is source of truth"
      principle as Cooking's, Food Ecosystem doc Section 17d/17a-2)
    - CANCELLATION with a defined input-return policy
    - MACHINE DESTRUCTION handling (breaking the station/machine
      running a job must not corrupt or duplicate the job's data —
      same requirement as Cooking's Section 17l)
    - OWNERSHIP (owner-locked by default, same proposed-not-permanent
      default as Cooking's, Food Ecosystem doc Section 17l)
    - AUTOMATION SOURCE tracking (Section above)
    - FAILURE handling
    - TRANSACTIONAL output creation (never partial)

  Each domain (Cooking, Chemistry, Industrial) still owns its OWN
  stage definitions, roll/check mechanics (if any), and domain-
  specific completion rules — this shared lifecycle is purely the
  bookkeeping scaffold underneath, not a constraint on what happens
  inside each stage.

5. EXPOSURE AND HAZARD BOUNDARY
================================================================================
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5a. Ownership split
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SAFETY (Food Ecosystem doc Section 6b) identifies WHAT hazards exist
    on a material/substance/sample — `SafetyProfile(hazards)`, derived
    `SafetyLevel`. This is already fully designed; Exposure builds on
    top of it, doesn't replace it.

  EXPOSURE (new, this document) resolves HOW a hazard actually reaches
    a target — route, dose, duration.

  public record ExposureEvent(
          UUID targetId, Identifier hazardId, Identifier route,
          float dose, int durationTicks
  ) {}

  Routes (identifiers, extensible): totality:ingestion,
  totality:inhalation, totality:skin_contact, totality:injection,
  totality:radiation, totality:magical_exposure — and further routes
  can be added later without an API change, same extensible-identifier
  pattern used everywhere else in Totality's design.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5b. RPG systems own the CONSEQUENCE, not Exposure itself
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Exposure only determines THAT a target was exposed, via what route,
  at what dose, for how long. What actually HAPPENS as a result —
  resistances, immunities, species traits, protective equipment,
  saving throws, status effect consequences — is owned by the RPG
  systems (attributes, saving throws, equipment, species traits),
  reading the ExposureEvent as their INPUT. Exposure doesn't itself
  decide "and now you take damage" — it hands off a fully-described
  exposure to whatever system resolves consequences.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5c. Disease owns what happens AFTER pathogen exposure specifically
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Once an ExposureEvent with a pathogen-type hazard resolves (per 5b's
  RPG-owned consequence check — e.g. a failed saving throw), DISEASE
  (TOTALITY_DISEASE_SPELL_INTERVENTION_DESIGN.txt) takes over for
  everything about infection and progression from that point forward.
  Exposure's job ends at "you were exposed and the RPG check
  determined this took hold" — Disease owns the ENTIRE lifecycle after
  that (stages, progression, treatment interactions, per that
  document's existing DiseaseDefinition/RestorationInteraction-now-
  InterventionResponseDefinition contracts).

6. CONTAINER AND TRANSFER COMPATIBILITY
================================================================================
Generalizes Fluid Material's `FluidPortDefinition` (Food Ecosystem doc
Section 9g) to cover more than just the phase/trait/temperature/
pressure fields already defined there — containers, tanks, pipes, and
machine ports may ADDITIONALLY declare:

  public record ContainerCompatibilityProfile(
          Set<FluidPhase> acceptedPhases,   -- reuses Food Ecosystem
                                                doc Section 9a's
                                                FluidPhase enum
          float capacity, float maxPressure, float maxTemperature,
          float corrosionResistance,
          Set<Identifier> reactivityCompatibility,
                                    -- which substances/materials this
                                       container is safe to hold
                                       without a reaction occurring
          boolean handlesSlurry, boolean sterile,
          boolean magicalContainment, boolean radiationShielding
  ) {}

  This EXTENDS (doesn't replace) `FluidPortDefinition` — think of
  `ContainerCompatibilityProfile` as the fuller-featured version for
  contexts (Chemistry apparatus, Industrial machinery) that need more
  than Fluid Material's base port fields, while ordinary Fluid
  Material pipes/pumps can keep using the simpler existing type.

  EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE for this document: the full LOGISTICS system
  (automated item/fluid routing, sorting, network-wide throughput
  planning) is NOT designed here — this section only establishes what
  a container/port CAN declare about its own compatibility, not how a
  broader logistics network moves things between them.

7. PHASE TRANSITIONS
================================================================================
Data-driven, shared definition for state changes: melting, freezing,
boiling, condensation, sublimation, crystallization, dissolving,
precipitation, casting, and solidification.

  public record PhaseTransitionDefinition(
          Identifier id, Identifier fromMaterialId, Identifier toMaterialId,
          Identifier transitionType,   -- totality:melting,
                                           totality:freezing,
                                           totality:boiling,
                                           totality:condensation,
                                           totality:sublimation,
                                           totality:crystallization,
                                           totality:dissolving,
                                           totality:precipitation,
                                           totality:casting,
                                           totality:solidification
          float triggerTemperature, Optional<Float> triggerPressure,
          Optional<Identifier> requiredCapability
  ) {}

  OWNERSHIP: MaterialDefinition/FluidMaterialDefinition (Section 1 /
  Food Ecosystem doc Section 9a) DESCRIBE which states a material CAN
  validly be in and roughly under what conditions — they don't
  themselves EXECUTE a transition. PROCESSING (Food Ecosystem doc
  Section 7) or a specific MACHINE executes the actual transition when
  triggered (e.g. a Boiler executing a boiling PhaseTransitionDefinition
  for Water->Steam — this is the SAME transition already described
  narratively in Food Ecosystem doc Section 9c, now given a real data
  shape here rather than being Gas-module-specific prose).

8. POLLUTION, WASTE, AND EMISSIONS
================================================================================
  Standardized hooks — NOT a full Pollution system design (that's
  future work, "a future Pollution service") — for: spills, waste
  fluids, smoke, toxic gases, water contamination, soil contamination,
  radiation, and magical corruption.

  public record PollutionEmissionEvent(
          Identifier sourceId, Identifier pollutionType,
          BlockPos origin, float intensity
  ) {}

  A FUTURE Pollution service owns REGIONAL APPLICATION and cleanup —
  not designed here, just flagged as the eventual owner of what happens
  after an emission event fires. ENVIRONMENT (Food Ecosystem doc
  Section 5) exposes the RESULTING state (e.g. `EnvironmentContext.
  tags` picking up `totality:polluted`, already anticipated as an
  example tag in that document) — Pollution writes into Environment's
  existing model rather than maintaining a parallel environmental-
  state system.

  PREFERENCE (not yet mandatory since the full system isn't designed):
  prefer CHUNK/REGION-level simulation over per-block simulation for
  pollution spread, same performance-conscious philosophy already
  established for Gas's world-simulation approach (Food Ecosystem doc
  Section 9c — avoid a full per-block simulation unless genuinely
  needed).

9. MACHINE CONDITION HOOKS
================================================================================
  FUTURE machine condition may eventually include: integrity, wear,
  corrosion, fouling, clogging, lubrication, calibration, overheating,
  and overpressure. NONE of this is designed here — full design
  explicitly WAITS for the Industrial document review (per Stefan's
  stated plan to show the RPG and Industrial specs next).

  The ONE thing locked in now, since it affects how OTHER systems
  should already emit events: FLUID MATERIAL and CHEMISTRY EMIT
  machine-impact events (e.g. "this contaminated Diesel just ran
  through you, here's the residue/wear implication") — they do NOT
  directly own or mutate machine durability themselves. Whatever the
  eventual Machine Condition system looks like, it listens for these
  events the same way Codex listens for DiscoveryEvents (Food
  Ecosystem doc Section 4c) — emit now, consume later, rather than
  Fluid Material/Chemistry needing to wait for Machine Condition to
  exist before they can describe their own effects.

10. KNOWLEDGE AND UNLOCK SEPARATION
================================================================================
  CODEX (Food Ecosystem doc Section 4) stores DISCOVERED INFORMATION —
  what the player has observed/learned ABOUT something. This is
  already fully designed and unchanged by this document.

  KNOWLEDGE/UNLOCK (this section, generalizing a pattern already used
  for Cooking's RecipeKnowledgeComponent, Food Ecosystem doc Section
  17a-2, and Disease/Spell's SpellUnlockDefinition/UnlockRequirement)
  stores PERMISSIONS — whether the player can actually USE/CRAFT/
  PERFORM something: recipes, chemical reactions, schematics,
  techniques, research results.

  RULE (important, prevents a common design trap): CODEX IS NOT THE
  UNIVERSAL PERMISSION GATE. Knowing ABOUT something (Codex) and being
  ALLOWED to make/use it (Knowledge/Unlock) are different questions
  with different answers — a player can be fully aware a recipe/
  reaction/schematic exists (high Codex discovery level) while still
  being completely unable to perform it (no Knowledge/Unlock grant),
  and vice versa in unusual cases (a hidden technique the player can
  already perform via some other unlock path without ever having
  formally "discovered" it in the Codex sense).

  Quests, NPCs, books, experimentation, and research may all GRANT
  knowledge/unlock — same multi-path philosophy as Disease/Spell's
  `UnlockRequirement` AllOf/AnyOf/Condition expression tree (see that
  document's corrections) — reused here as the general-purpose
  mechanism for ANY domain's unlock requirements (Chemistry reactions,
  Smithing schematics, Cooking recipes, Spell acquisition, species
  unlocks, dimensional gates, artifact attunement all share this ONE
  requirement-expression type rather than each domain inventing its
  own).
